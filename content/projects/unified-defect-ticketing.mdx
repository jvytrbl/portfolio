## Problem

Defect reports at a fabric manufacturing company were spread across multiple WhatsApp groups and
manual logs. Nothing was traceable end to end, and every department kept its own partial picture.

## Solution

A single ticketing system, now used daily by **sales, warehouse, accounts, and purchasing**. As the
sole engineer I own architecture, deployment, and ongoing maintenance, and work directly with end
users to turn real operational workflows into system design.

## Architecture

```txt
Field sales PWA (mobile-first) ─┐
                                ├──► Supabase (Postgres + real-time sync)
Operations dashboard (desktop) ─┘            │
                                             ▼
                    ERP pipeline ──► Microsoft Dynamics Business Central
                                     (service orders · credit notes)
```

- **Two Next.js apps, one backend:** a mobile-first PWA for field sales and a desktop dashboard
  for internal operations, kept in sync in real time.
- **Multi-tenant, role-based access** supporting multiple business units, with Microsoft Azure AD
  single sign-on.
- **ERP integration** with Business Central for automated service order and credit note
  generation.

## Scaling the data browser

A data browser holding **90,000+ records** started causing browser stability failures. After
diagnosing the root cause as client-side loading at scale, I redesigned it to use server-side
pagination, keeping the UI responsive regardless of dataset size.

## Outcomes

- One shared, traceable record of every defect across four departments.
- ERP documents generated automatically instead of re-keyed by hand.
- A data browser that stays stable as records grow.
